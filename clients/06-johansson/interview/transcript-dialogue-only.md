## Transcript Notes

This is one of the most emotionally complex transcripts. Linda is a 71-year-old widow whose primary planning concern involves protecting her son Peter from himself during a potential relapse, while emphatically not wanting to punish him or include degrading language like sobriety testing provisions. The transcript tests the system's ability to capture nuanced emotional reasoning alongside precise legal instructions.

Additional complexity: Linda has an existing trust from 2015 that needs to be restated (not revoked and replaced — she specifically wants continuity). The Coronado house was transferred out of the trust after Erik's death and needs to be re-titled. The IRA beneficiary designation may conflict with the new plan depending on how Peter's discretionary trust is structured.

Linda speaks carefully and deliberately, often pausing to consider her words. She gets emotional when discussing Peter's struggles but is resolute about her decisions.

**[04:00] — Family Structure**

**ATTORNEY:** Tell me about the rest of your family.

**LINDA:** I have a son. Peter. He's 43, lives in Seattle. Peter is... Peter has struggled with alcohol. For a long time. He's been in and out of programs. We can set up what's called a discretionary trust for his share, where the trustee has the authority to manage distributions based on Peter's needs and circumstances.

**LINDA:** Yes. That's what I want. But I do not — and I want to be very clear about this — I do not want any language in the document about drug testing or sobriety checks or any of that. **ATTORNEY:** Understood completely. No sobriety testing, no specific conditions related to substance use. The trustee just has judgment.

**LINDA:** Yes. Exactly.

**[08:30] — The Existing Trust**

**ATTORNEY:** Linda, you mentioned updating things since Erik passed. Do you have an existing estate plan?

*[She gives a small smile]*

**LINDA:** After Erik died, his share of the trust was distributed to me according to the trust terms. Everything came to me. So the trust is still there, in my name, but it's still set up the old way — everything to the surviving spouse, then equally to the children outright.

**LINDA:** Yes. I want to keep the trust — not start a brand new one. I want to restate it. My attorney at the time used that word — "restate." **ATTORNEY:** A restatement is the right approach. It preserves the trust entity and its history while updating the provisions. Now, you mentioned property assignments — what assets are currently in the trust?

**ATTORNEY:** That's important to address. If the house isn't titled in the trust, it would go through probate, which is exactly what the trust is supposed to avoid. We'll need to transfer the house back into the trust as part of this process.

**LINDA:** Yes, please. That's something I've been meaning to do for years.
