# Clients, by wealth tier

Clients are grouped by the tier of wealth they sit in, because the tier decides what the planning is about and therefore what a client at that tier tests. Tier 1 is populated. Tiers 2 to 4 are empty and waiting for contributors; each folder's README says what belongs there.

| Tier | Folder | Rough range | What defines it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [`tier-1-foundational/`](tier-1-foundational/) | Up to about $3.5M | No tax exposure. The plan is the core document set; the hard parts are people. |
| 2 | [`tier-2-affluent/`](tier-2-affluent/) | About $3.5M up to the federal exemption | Still non-taxable, but no longer comfortably. Multiple properties, a closely held business, concentrated stock, large retirement accounts. |
| 3 | [`tier-3-taxable/`](tier-3-taxable/) | The federal exemption up to about $50M | Federal estate tax is a real cost. Planning is tax-driven and the attorney works alongside a CPA and advisor. |
| 4 | [`tier-4-ultra-high-net-worth/`](tier-4-ultra-high-net-worth/) | Above about $50M | Family office or on the way to one. Multi-generational governance, several jurisdictions, often international family or assets. |

**The boundaries are relative to the federal estate tax exemption**, not fixed dollars. In 2026 the exemption is $15M per person, indexed, so $30M for a married couple with portability. If the exemption moves, the tier 2 and tier 3 boundary moves with it. Net worth alone does not decide a borderline case: a $4M estate that is a house and an IRA is tier 1, and a $3M estate that is a business with partners and a rental in another state is tier 2. Place the client where the planning problems belong.

**Numbering runs across the whole set, not per tier.** Clients 01 to 09 are tier 1; the next client is 10 whatever tier it joins. `COVERAGE.md` refers to clients by number and the numbers must stay unique.
