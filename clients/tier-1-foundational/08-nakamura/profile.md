### Planning Goals
**Primary Concern:** A comprehensive plan that provides for each other,
then for the children fairly, and leaves a meaningful charitable legacy.
George as a CPA is detail-oriented about tax efficiency.

**Distribution:** Everything to the surviving spouse. After both have
passed:

-   \$25,000 for pet trust for Koda. Christine to be caretaker.

-   \$50,000 each to grandchildren Aiden and Olivia's 529 plans.

-   George's traditional IRA directly to four charities (Japanese
    American National Museum, San Diego Buddhist Temple, SDSU
    scholarship fund, Doctors Without Borders) split equally.
    Tax-efficient since distributions from a traditional IRA to charity
    avoid income tax.

-   George's Roth IRA to the trust (Roth distributions aren't taxable to
    beneficiaries, so better to go to the family).

-   Remaining trust assets split equally between Christine and Ryan.

**Ryan's Partner:** George and Patricia adore Sam and want to
acknowledge him, but aren't sure how to do so in the trust without
creating complications. They're considering a specific bequest of
\$20,000 to Sam with a letter expressing their feelings separately from
the legal document.

**Trustee:** Surviving spouse. Successor: Christine. Second successor:
George's brother Frank (he's a retired banker and understands finances).

**Pet Provision:** \$25,000 for Koda. Christine primary caretaker.
Backup: Ryan.

**DPOA Agent:** Each spouse names the other. George's successor agent:
Christine. Patricia's successor agent: Margaret (her sister).

**DPOA Type:** Springing for both.

**Body Disposition:** Both wish to be cremated. George wants ashes

### Prior Legal Context
George and Patricia had simple wills drawn up in the early 1990s when
Christine was born. They never created a trust. The wills left
everything to the surviving spouse and then equally to the children.
These are significantly outdated. George's IRA currently lists Patricia
as the primary beneficiary and the children as contingent beneficiaries
equally --- this doesn't align with the new plan to direct the
traditional IRA to charities.

George's CPA practice has no formal succession plan but this isn't a
major concern since he's winding down and the practice has minimal
tangible asset value.
