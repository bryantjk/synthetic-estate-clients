### Family Structure
Ray's social circle has narrowed significantly in recent years. His
partner Walter, with whom he lived for 28 years, passed away in 2018
from cancer. Ray was Walter's primary caregiver during the illness, and
the experience is a major motivator for getting his own affairs in
order.

-   No children.

-   One surviving sibling: Brother, Gerald Delacroix, age 78, lives in a
    memory care facility in Baton Rouge, LA. Gerald has advanced
    dementia and is unable to serve in any fiduciary capacity. Gerald
    has two adult children (Ray's nephew and niece): Paul Delacroix, age
    50, lives in Houston, TX, and Marie Delacroix-Tran, age 47, lives in
    Baton Rouge, LA. Ray has a cordial but not close relationship with
    both --- they exchange holiday cards and occasional phone calls.

-   Deceased partner Walter had no surviving family.

-   Ray's closest friend is Evelyn Marsh, age 71, a former colleague
    from the library. They have lunch weekly. Evelyn has her own health
    issues (recent hip replacement, early-stage macular degeneration)
    and has told Ray she would not be comfortable serving as his trustee
    or agent.

-   Ray also has a friendly relationship with his neighbor, Joseph Kim,
    age 45, who checks on him regularly and helps with things around the
    condo. Joseph is willing to be an emergency contact but has
    explicitly said he doesn't want legal responsibility.

-   Cat: Cleo, age 9

### Planning Goals
**Primary Concern:** Ray watched Walter die without a proper estate
plan, and he went through a painful experience trying to manage Walter's
affairs without legal authority (they never formalized their
relationship or created powers of attorney). He is deeply motivated to
make sure whoever manages his affairs has clear legal authority and that
the process is as smooth as possible.

**Secondary Concern:** He has no one in his personal life who is both
willing and able to serve as trustee, executor, or DPOA agent. He has
accepted this reality and is pragmatic about it, but it saddens him. He
wants to find a professional fiduciary he can trust.

**Distribution:**

-   Walter's artwork to the San Diego Public Library for display in the
    central branch --- Ray wants Walter's memory preserved publicly.

-   Rare book collection to the SDPL as well, with a request that they
    be maintained as a named collection (the "Delacroix-Hartman
    Collection," using both Ray's and Walter's surnames).

-   \$15,000 to a pet trust for Cleo.

-   \$10,000 each to nephew Paul and niece Marie.

-   \$10,000 to Evelyn Marsh.

-   \$5,000 to Joseph Kim as a thank-you for his kindness.

-   Remaining assets split: 50% to the San Diego LGBT Community Center,
    30% to the San Diego Public Library Foundation, 20% to an AIDS
    memorial fund in Walter's name (fund already established at the San
    Diego Foundation).

**Professional Fiduciary:** Ray has been referred to a licensed
professional fiduciary in San Diego to serve as trustee, executor, and
DPOA agent. He has not yet selected one but understands they charge fees
based on a percentage of assets under management or hourly rates. He
wants the trust document to include provisions for reasonable
compensation and a clear process for replacing the fiduciary if needed.

**Trustee:** Professional fiduciary (to be named). The trust should
include a trust protector provision allowing a designated person to
remove and replace the professional fiduciary if they are not performing
adequately.

**Trust Protector:** Evelyn Marsh, with the understanding that this role
requires minimal active involvement --- she would only need to act if
the fiduciary was failing in their duties. If Evelyn is unable, Ray is
open to naming the San Diego LGBT Community Center's executive director
in an institutional capacity.

**Pet Provision:** \$15,000 for Cleo's care. Joseph Kim has agreed to
take Cleo if something happens to Ray. Backup: the San Diego Humane
Society.

**DPOA Agent:** Professional fiduciary (to be named). Ray wants both
financial and healthcare decision-making authority with the same
fiduciary for simplicity.

**DPOA Type:** Springing. Ray is currently in good health and
cognitively sharp. He wants to retain full autonomy as long as possible.

**Body Disposition:** Cremation. Ashes to be scattered at Balboa Park
near the botanical garden where he and Walter used to walk. If not
permitted, interment at Greenwood Memorial Park.

### Prior Legal Context
Ray has no prior estate planning documents --- no trust, no will, no
powers of attorney. He and Walter never created any legal documents
formalizing their relationship or planning for incapacity, which led to
significant difficulties when Walter became ill. Walter's estranged
family initially contested Ray's involvement in medical decisions, and
Ray had to petition for conservatorship --- a costly and emotionally
devastating process that took months. This experience is the central
motivation for Ray's planning.

Ray's IRA currently has no beneficiary designation on file (the original
designation named Walter and was never updated after Walter's death).
His credit union accounts also have no payable-on-death designations.
The condo has no transfer-on-death deed.

### Testing Value
This profile tests a critical scenario: a client with no viable personal
candidates for fiduciary roles. The capture engine must recognize that
the professional fiduciary is not yet selected (a "to be named"
placeholder), handle the trust protector concept (which adds a
supervisory layer over the professional fiduciary), and capture the
specific compensation and replacement provisions that are unique to
professional fiduciary arrangements. The emotional backstory about
Walter is important context the system should capture, as it explains
the urgency and specific concerns driving the plan. Also tests multiple
beneficiary designations that are either missing or outdated, the
concept of a named collection as a bequest, and institutional
beneficiaries serving in backup fiduciary roles.
