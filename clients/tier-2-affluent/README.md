# Tier 2: affluent, non-taxable

**Rough range:** about $3.5M up to the federal exemption ($15M per person in 2026, $30M for a married couple with portability). **Populated:** no clients yet.

The estate is still under the exemption, but no longer comfortably. Growth or a change in the law could push it over, so the planning is exemption-aware without being tax-driven. What changes from tier 1 is the asset side: there is more of it, it is held in more forms, and some of it carries liability.

**What defines a tier 2 client:** more than one piece of real property, often a rental or a vacation home and sometimes out of state; a closely held business with an operating agreement; concentrated stock or equity compensation; retirement accounts large enough that the SECURE Act ten-year payout rule shapes who should inherit them; life insurance large enough to want an irrevocable trust.

**What the planning turns on:** asset protection (LLCs for rentals, umbrella coverage), SECURE Act payout planning, irrevocable life insurance trusts, portability elections, corporate or professional trustees, ancillary probate avoidance for out-of-state property, and a plan that can absorb a reduced exemption without being rewritten.

**What a tier 2 client tests that tier 1 does not:** reading entity structures and asset schedules with many line items; a client who arrives with existing documents and entities the system must read rather than start from scratch; tax vocabulary used by the client, sometimes incorrectly; a plan that is a design choice rather than a fixed five-document set.

**Shapes this tier would most usefully add first** (from the gaps in `COVERAGE.md`): a business with partners and a buy-sell agreement, real property in more than one state, a prior plan from another firm's drafting system to be restated, a second marriage with children on both sides.
